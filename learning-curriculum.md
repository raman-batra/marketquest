# Learning Plan: From Zero to First Real Trade

## How to Read This Document

This is a **12-week curriculum** to take you from "I don't know what a stop-loss is" to "I have placed 50 paper trades, journaled each one, and understand my own edge (or lack of one)." It's structured by week, with specific hours, specific resources, and specific deliverables.

**One rule above all others: do not skip ahead to live trading.** The curriculum is designed so that by the time you place a real trade, you have already lost (paper) money in dozens of ways and understood why. That experience is what survives contact with real money.

If 12 weeks feels like too long, multiply by your overconfidence: that's how long it'll actually take. Most people who survive long-term spent 1-2 years before they were consistently profitable. This plan compresses the structured part of that into 12 weeks.

---

## The Five Pillars (and Why They're in This Order)

Most beginners learn in the wrong order. They start with "what stocks should I buy?" and end with risk management — usually after blowing up. The right order is:

1. **Markets & Mechanics** — what you're actually buying, how orders work, what the players are
2. **Risk Management & Position Sizing** — the only thing that keeps you alive
3. **Psychology & Process** — the actual reason most people lose
4. **Analysis (Fundamental + Technical)** — *now* we can talk about what to buy
5. **Execution & Iteration** — paper trading, journaling, refining

The math: a trader with mediocre analysis but excellent risk management beats a brilliant analyst with no risk discipline, every time.

## Pre-Curriculum Prerequisites (Before Week 1)

Don't start until you have:

### Financial readiness
- ✅ **Emergency fund** = 6 months of expenses, in a savings account or liquid fund. **Untouchable.**
- ✅ **All high-interest debt paid off** (credit cards, personal loans). Returns from trading rarely beat 18% interest.
- ✅ **Risk capital identified**: ₹25,000–₹50,000 you can lose 100% of without changing your life. Not borrowed, not from your emergency fund, not from your kid's education fund.
- ✅ **Term life insurance + health insurance** in place. If your dependents need your trading capital to survive a hospital bill, you can't trade properly.
- ✅ **Existing SIPs in index funds** (Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50, or similar). This is your real wealth builder. Trading is the side experiment.

If any of the above is missing, **fix that first**. Trading on a shaky financial foundation is just acceleration toward a worse outcome.

### Skill prerequisites
- ✅ **Basic English reading comprehension** (most quality material is in English)
- ✅ **Basic spreadsheet skills** (Excel/Google Sheets — for trade journal, position sizing calc)
- ✅ **Math comfort with percentages, ratios, basic statistics** (not calculus — just averages, std dev, percentages)
- ✅ **2-3 hours/day of focused learning time** for 12 weeks. If you can't carve this out, the plan won't work.

### Tools to set up (Day 0 — 2 hours)
- ✅ A dedicated trading email (separate from personal/work)
- ✅ A password manager (Bitwarden free, 1Password)
- ✅ A note-taking system (Notion, Obsidian, or even plain Markdown files)
- ✅ A blank Google Sheet titled "Trade Journal" — we'll structure it in Week 2

### What NOT to do during the curriculum
- ❌ Don't open a broker account in Week 1. You'll be tempted to trade. Wait until Week 4.
- ❌ Don't join any "tips" Telegram channels or paid groups. Zero. Not even "just to see."
- ❌ Don't watch random "stock predictions" YouTube videos. You'll absorb noise.
- ❌ Don't read trading Twitter/X. Survivor bias is brutal there.
- ❌ Don't tell friends/family what you're doing yet. Pressure to look successful is the enemy of disciplined learning.

## Week-by-Week Plan

The format for each week:
- **Goal** — what you should be able to do by end of week
- **Reading** — primary resource(s)
- **Watching** — specific videos
- **Doing** — exercises
- **Deliverable** — proof you absorbed it

Estimated time: **12-15 hours/week**.

### WEEK 1 — Markets, Mechanics, and the Big Picture

**Goal:** Understand what a stock actually is, how exchanges work, who the players are, what an order does, and what fees exist.

**Reading (8h):**
- Zerodha Varsity, **Module 1: Introduction to Stock Markets** (entire module, all chapters). This is the gold standard for Indian beginners. Free, no signup. URL: `zerodha.com/varsity/modules/`
  - Read every chapter. Take notes by hand or in your note system.
  - Do the end-of-chapter quizzes.

**Watching (2h):**
- Zerodha Varsity YouTube channel — videos that match Module 1
- **NSE India's investor education videos** on `nseindia.com` — short, official, accurate

**Doing (2h):**
- Open a **demo account on Tickertape** (free) — explore stock pages for 5 companies you know (Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank, ITC, Maruti). Read the financials section. Don't worry about understanding everything yet.
- On NSE India, find one corporate filing (an earnings result PDF) and try to read the headline numbers.
- Build a **glossary file** with every new term. Aim for 50+ terms by end of week: bid-ask, market cap, free float, P/E, EPS, dividend, demat, T+1, circuit limit, STT, brokerage, Nifty, Sensex, sector index, IPO, FPO, OFS, buyback…

**Deliverable:**
- Can you explain to a friend, in 5 minutes, the difference between primary and secondary markets, and what a stockbroker actually does?
- Do you know why Indian markets are open 9:15-15:30 IST and what pre-open / post-close mean?

### WEEK 2 — Risk Management & Position Sizing (THE most important week)

**Goal:** Internalize that you are a risk manager who happens to trade, not a trader who manages risk.

**Reading (5h):**
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** by Van K. Tharp — Chapters 1-6 specifically (you'll finish the rest later, but the core concepts of expectancy, position sizing, and R-multiples are in these). Buy used or borrow. **This single book has saved more retail traders than any technical analysis course.**
- Zerodha Varsity, **Module 9: Risk Management & Trading Psychology** — Chapter 1-4

**Watching (3h):**
- Search YouTube for "Van Tharp expectancy" — watch any 30-min video that explains the formula
- Search "Adam Khoo position sizing" — clearer explanations than most Indian channels for this topic
- For Indian context: **Trading Q&A by Zerodha** has good threads on position sizing — search "position sizing"

**Doing (5h):**
- **Build a position sizing calculator in Google Sheets.** Inputs: account size, risk %, entry price, stop-loss price. Outputs: position size in shares, capital deployed, max loss in ₹. This is your single most-used tool going forward.
- **Compute the math of ruin**: if you risk 5% per trade and have a 50% win rate, what's the probability you blow up after 20 losing trades? (Answer: very high.) Now redo with 1% risk. (Answer: minimal.) Make a table. Internalize it.
- **Calculate R-multiples for 10 hypothetical trades.** Read about it in Tharp's book. R = your initial risk per trade. A trade that makes 3× your risk = +3R. A trade that hits stop = -1R. Your job is to find a system with positive expectancy in R.

**Deliverable:**
- Position sizing calculator that you can show me and explain
- Written 1-page rules document with your own answers to: "What % of my capital will I risk per trade? What's my max daily loss? What's my max concurrent positions? What happens if I hit my daily loss limit?"

This week's content matters more than the next 10 weeks combined. **Don't move on until this clicks.**

---

### WEEK 3 — Trading Psychology

**Goal:** Understand why your brain is the worst part of your trading edge, and what to do about it.

**Reading (8h):**
- **Trading in the Zone** by Mark Douglas — read it slowly, take notes. This is the #1 trading psychology book ever written. The core insight: "Anything can happen, and you don't need to know what's going to happen next to make money."
- For Indian context, read Karthik Rangappa's Varsity Module 9 chapters on psychology

**Watching (2h):**
- Mark Douglas's recorded talks on YouTube (search "Mark Douglas trading in the zone full")
- "Behavioral biases in trading" — any decent explainer; understand: confirmation bias, recency bias, sunk cost fallacy, loss aversion, gambler's fallacy, FOMO

**Doing (3h):**
- **Honest self-assessment:** Write down (privately) 3 financial decisions you've regretted, what emotion drove them, and what you'd do differently. This is uncomfortable. It's supposed to be.
- **Pre-write your "worst case" plan:** "When I am down 5% in a day, I will [specific action]. When I have 5 losing trades in a row, I will [specific action]." Have these on paper *before* you ever need them.
- Read the SEBI Telegram bust cases (e.g., Yash Trading Academy, Intraday Jackpot). Understand emotionally why people fell for them. You will be tempted in the same way.

**Deliverable:**
- Personal trading rules document v1 — combine Week 2 risk rules + Week 3 psychology rules. Print it. Tape it above your desk.

### WEEK 4 — Open Broker Account, Set Up Tools, Start Paper Trading

**Goal:** Have a working trading setup, but commit to **paper-only** for 6 weeks.

**Doing (10h):**
- **Open a Zerodha account** (or Dhan if you prefer the algo/API focus). Account opening takes 24-48 hours.
- Set up Kite (web + mobile). Explore. Place zero real orders.
- Set up these free accounts:
  - **TradingView** (free tier) — best charting
  - **Screener.in** — fundamental data on every Indian stock
  - **Tickertape** free tier
  - **Sensibull** — for options understanding (don't trade them yet)
  - **Pulse by Zerodha** — bookmark for news
- Set up **paper trading**:
  - TradingView's paper trading mode (built-in)
  - OR Sensibull's virtual trading
  - OR Moneybhai by Moneycontrol
- **Build your trade journal** in Google Sheets with these columns:
  - Date | Symbol | Setup/Strategy | Entry | Stop-loss | Target | Position size (shares) | ₹ at risk | R-multiple result | Win/Loss | Reason for entry | Reason for exit | Emotion (1-10 stress) | What I'd do differently | Notes

**Reading (4h):**
- Zerodha Varsity **Module 2: Technical Analysis** — Chapters 1-5 (start of TA — chart types, candlesticks, support/resistance)

**Deliverable:**
- Working broker account (no real trades yet)
- Trade journal template ready
- 5 paper trades placed (small, just to learn the interface)

### WEEK 5-6 — Technical Analysis: The Useful 20%

**Goal:** Know enough TA to identify trend, support/resistance, and a few specific setups. Not 47 indicators.

The dirty secret of TA: 80% of indicators are noise. The useful 20%:
- Price action (candlesticks, structure)
- Trend (higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows; one moving average is enough)
- Support & resistance
- Volume confirmation
- One or two indicators max (e.g., RSI for momentum, ATR for volatility)

**Reading (10h over 2 weeks):**
- Zerodha Varsity **Module 2: Technical Analysis** — finish entire module. Karthik does it well; cleaner than most YouTube content.
- Optional: **John Murphy's "Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets"** — the textbook. Skim, don't memorize. Reference book, not a sit-down read.

**Watching (4h over 2 weeks):**
- **Power of Stocks** YouTube channel — purely price action; watch their setup videos. Subhasish Pani is good at this.
- For chart patterns: **Trading Chanakya** (Indian context) — search his videos on candlestick patterns and trend lines

**Doing (10h over 2 weeks):**
- **Open 100 charts** of Nifty 50 stocks on TradingView. For each, do this in 2 minutes:
  - Draw the trend (up/down/sideways)
  - Mark 2 support and 2 resistance levels
  - Note the most recent candlestick pattern, if any
- After 100 charts, you'll start to see patterns naturally. There's no shortcut — chart hours are the cost of pattern recognition.
- **Place 10 paper trades using ONE setup only.** Pick the simplest: "buy on bounce from support in an uptrend, stop below support, target previous resistance." Don't change setup yet. Repetition > variety.

**Deliverable:**
- 10 paper trades journaled with specific support/resistance reasoning
- A 1-page document describing your single chosen setup with rules: entry condition, stop placement, target placement, what invalidates the setup

---

### WEEK 7 — Fundamental Analysis: What's a Business Worth?

**Goal:** Read a balance sheet and P&L well enough to know if a company is healthy. Understand basic valuation.

You don't need to be a CFA to trade. But you need to know if "RELIANCE" is a business or a meme. Most retail traders skip this and end up trading penny-stock pump-and-dumps because they don't know the difference.

**Reading (10h):**
- Zerodha Varsity **Module 3: Fundamental Analysis** — entire module
- Optional: **The Intelligent Investor** by Benjamin Graham — Chapters 8 (Mr. Market) and 20 (Margin of Safety) only. Skip the rest unless you love value investing.
- For Indian context: Pranjal Kamra's Finology videos on reading annual reports

**Watching (3h):**
- **CA Rachana Phadke Ranade** — her videos on balance sheet, P&L, and ratios. Solid for Indian beginners.

**Doing (5h):**
- Pick **5 companies** in different sectors (e.g., Reliance, HDFC Bank, ITC, Tata Motors, TCS).
- For each, on Screener.in:
  - Look at 10-year revenue, profit, ROE, ROCE, debt
  - Read the latest annual report's "Letter from Chairman" section
  - Compute P/E, P/B, debt-to-equity yourself
  - Form a one-line opinion: "Healthy & growing / healthy & flat / declining / red flag"
- Now understand: even strong fundamental companies can have terrible price action for years. FA tells you *what* to buy. TA tells you *when*.

**Deliverable:**
- 5 one-page company snapshots with your own analysis
- A working ability to pull up any Indian stock on Screener.in and form a 30-second judgment

### WEEK 8 — Putting It Together: Develop Your First Strategy

**Goal:** A written, testable, simple strategy. Yours. Not copied from a YouTube guru.

A strategy must answer:
1. **Universe**: which stocks am I trading? (e.g., Nifty 50 only, or Nifty 200, or "stocks with mcap > ₹10,000 cr and not in F&O ban")
2. **Setup**: under what specific conditions do I enter? (e.g., "stock closes above 50-DMA for 3 days, RSI > 50, has just bounced from support")
3. **Entry**: at what price exactly? (e.g., "next day's open" or "stop above current day's high")
4. **Stop-loss**: where? (e.g., "below the most recent swing low" or "1 ATR below entry")
5. **Target**: where? (e.g., "next resistance" or "2× the risk")
6. **Position sizing**: how much? (e.g., "1% of capital risked per trade")
7. **Time horizon**: hold for how long? (e.g., "until target/stop hit, or 20 days max")
8. **What invalidates the strategy?**: when do you stop trading it?

**Reading (5h):**
- Tharp's **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** — the chapters on system development you skipped in Week 2

**Doing (10h):**
- **Backtest manually**: pick your strategy, look at 50 historical setups on past charts (TradingView's bar replay feature is great for this). Note each: did it hit stop or target? What's your win rate, avg R-multiple, profit factor?
- If win rate × avg win < loss rate × avg loss — strategy is unprofitable. Modify or pick a different one.
- Once you have a strategy that *appears* to work in manual backtesting, document it formally.

**Deliverable:**
- **Strategy Document v1** — 1-2 pages with all 8 elements above
- Manual backtest results: 50 historical setups, win rate, average winner, average loser, profit factor

---

### WEEK 9-10 — Disciplined Paper Trading

**Goal:** 50 paper trades using your one strategy, journaled. The point isn't profit — it's process.

**Doing (15h/week):**
- Trade only your documented strategy. Resist the urge to "just take this one good-looking setup" outside your rules.
- **Every trade gets journaled before market close**. Entry reason, stop, target, emotion, what's happening in the market that day.
- **Track your own behavior**, not just P&L: did you take every signal? Did you exit early on winners? Did you widen stops on losers? Did you size bigger after 3 wins?
- **Weekly review every Sunday**: pull up the journal. What patterns emerge in your *behavior*? Not the trades — your behavior.

**Reading (4h/week):**
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** by Edwin Lefèvre — for context. Old book, eternally relevant. Read 30 minutes/day.

**Deliverable:**
- 50 paper trades journaled
- Honest self-evaluation: am I following my rules? Where am I deviating? Why?

---

### WEEK 11 — Stress Test & Refine

**Goal:** Understand how your strategy performs in different market conditions before trusting it with real money.

**Doing (10h):**
- Look at your 50 trades. Group them by market condition:
  - Trending up
  - Trending down
  - Sideways
- Note: did your strategy work in all three? Most don't. SMA crossover dies in sideways markets. Mean reversion dies in strong trends. **Knowing when your strategy fails is more valuable than knowing when it works.**
- **Run your strategy through historical "shock" periods**: March 2020 (Covid), Jan 2008, May 2022, August 2024 vol spike. How would it have performed? Use TradingView bar replay.
- Identify the worst losing streak you'd have endured. Could you have stayed disciplined through it?

**Reading (5h):**
- Karthik Rangappa's **Varsity Module 5: Options Strategies** OR **Module 6: Option Theory** — *only if* you're considering F&O eventually. Otherwise skip and go deeper into stock selection.

**Deliverable:**
- Strategy Document v2 with explicit "this strategy underperforms in [X] market conditions, so I will reduce size or pause when [Y]" rules
- A go/no-go decision: are you ready for real money?

If after 11 weeks, your paper trading is **net negative or breakeven**: do not go live yet. Most people aren't ready until 6-12 months in. There is no shame in extending paper trading. There is huge shame in rushing to live and blowing up.

---

### WEEK 12 — First Real Trades (Tiny)

**Goal:** Place 5 real trades using your strategy. Position sizes 25% of what you'd normally take. The point isn't profit — it's discovering what's different in real trading.

**Doing (10h):**
- Real money creates emotions paper trading doesn't. You will:
  - Want to exit winners early (lock in profit before it disappears)
  - Want to widen stops on losers (it'll come back!)
  - Skip valid signals (this one looks too risky)
  - Take invalid signals (FOMO)
- Notice these. Journal them. **Don't fight them — observe them.** Awareness is the first step.
- After 5 real trades, return to paper for 2 weeks if you didn't follow your rules.
- After 20 real trades that follow your rules, you can scale up position size to 50% of plan.
- After 50 real trades, you're at full position sizing.

**Deliverable:**
- 5 small real trades, each journaled with explicit comparison to "how would I have felt doing this in paper?"

## Optional but Valuable: NISM Certification

Even if you never want to be a research analyst, sitting for **NISM Series XV (Research Analyst)** as a self-imposed deadline is one of the best things you can do:

- The syllabus is the curriculum: securities markets, terminology, equity, debt, fundamentals, valuation, technical, corporate actions, ethics
- The exam (~₹1,500 fee) gives you a hard deadline to actually finish learning
- Passing it (60% needed, 25% negative marking) means you've genuinely absorbed the material
- It's a SEBI-recognized credential — useful even if just as motivation
- Updated curriculum effective Jan 20, 2026

You don't need certification to trade your own money. You do need the knowledge that the certification tests. Use it as a forcing function. Aim to pass by Week 10.

Other useful NISM exams (in order of relevance to traders):
- **Series VIII — Equity Derivatives** (if you want to trade F&O eventually)
- **Series VII — Securities Operations & Risk Management** (good operations knowledge)
- **Series V-A — Mutual Fund Distributors** (if you ever advise others)

## A Daily Routine (Once Curriculum Is Done)

This is what your day looks like after Week 12, ideally:

| Time (IST) | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-market 8:00-9:00 | Read overnight news (Pulse), check global markets, check economic calendar, plan day | 60 min |
| 9:00-9:15 | Check own watchlist, set alerts, review trade journal rules | 15 min |
| 9:15-15:30 | Market hours. Trade ONLY pre-planned setups. Don't stare at screen all day. | as needed |
| 15:30-16:00 | Close out, journal every trade (entry/exit reasons, emotion 1-10, lesson learned) | 30 min |
| Evening | Review charts of stocks of interest, study material, weekly backtest one new idea | 60-90 min |
| Sunday | Weekly review: P&L, rule adherence, what worked, what didn't, plan for next week | 90 min |

If you can't commit to this, you can't be a discretionary trader. That's fine — you can still build a Claude Code-driven systematic system that needs less daily attention.

## Self-Assessment Before Going Live

Before placing your first real-money trade, you should be able to honestly answer "yes" to all of these:

- [ ] I can explain expectancy and R-multiples to someone else
- [ ] I have a written, specific strategy document
- [ ] I have placed at least 50 paper trades using ONE strategy
- [ ] I have a working position sizing calculator
- [ ] I have specific, written rules for: max risk per trade, daily loss limit, max concurrent positions
- [ ] I have a trade journal habit — every trade journaled, no exceptions, for 6+ weeks
- [ ] I know exactly what would make me stop trading my strategy ("when X happens, I pause")
- [ ] I have read Trading in the Zone or equivalent psychology book
- [ ] I have an emergency fund and term insurance in place
- [ ] My trading capital is genuinely "lose 100% and life continues" money
- [ ] I have not joined a single paid Telegram tip channel
- [ ] I know what a 30% drawdown will *feel* like (have I imagined it?)
- [ ] I have someone I can talk to about losses without it being a crisis (spouse, friend, therapist)

Anything not checked? **Don't go live until it is.**

## What to Do When You Lose Money (You Will)

When (not if) you have a bad week:

1. **Stop trading immediately** if you've hit your weekly loss limit
2. **Don't try to "win it back."** This is the single most expensive emotion in trading.
3. **Review the journal**: were the trades themselves the problem, or was it execution?
4. **If trades followed your strategy and still lost**: it's expected variance. Continue.
5. **If trades violated your strategy**: you have a discipline problem, not a strategy problem. The fix is process, not better setups.
6. **Talk to someone.** Trading isolation amplifies bad decision-making.
7. **If you've lost > 20% of capital**: stop completely. Restart paper trading. Review whether you're temperamentally suited for active trading at all (many people aren't — and that's okay).

## What "Done" Looks Like

You're not "done" learning at Week 12. You're done with the structured curriculum. Real proficiency takes 3-5 years of live trading, with proper journaling and review.

What changes at Week 12 is that you have:
- A working system (mental + operational)
- A risk framework that protects you from yourself
- The vocabulary to keep learning
- The humility to know you don't know
- The patience to compound slowly rather than gamble fast

That last one is the actual edge. Most retail traders never get past wanting to make money fast. The ones who do, last.
